KARGO HIRING RUBRIC - Product Manager (PM) and Senior Product Manager (SPM)
Source: patterns in the 8 past hire profiles and their performance ratings.
The job descriptions were used only to understand what the roles involve.

=====================================================================
PATTERNS FROM THE HIRE PROFILES
=====================================================================
Exceeds: Rohan Desai, Sunita Krishnamurthy, Aditya Shetty, Meghna Tiwari, Lavanya Iyer
Meets:   Vikram Nair, Rahul Bose
Below:   Preetham Rao

Pattern 1 - They handled the workflow with their own hands before building,
selling or supporting software for it, and their later work fixes that pain.
  Seen in all 5 Exceeds hires. Rohan went from 180+ shipments a month, to an
  Excel tracker, to a freight tracking platform. Lavanya went from managing
  exceptions on 500+ live shipments to PM for freight visibility. Meghna
  cleared documentation errors and customs holds; Sunita handled 200+
  shipments of documentation a month; Aditya dealt with berth windows and
  detention disputes at JNPT.
  Missing in Vikram and Rahul. Preetham connected to 3PL APIs but never
  handled a shipment himself.

Pattern 2 - They spotted a broken process nobody gave them, built a rough fix
fast, and other people took it up without being told to.
  Seen in all 5 Exceeds hires. Rohan's Excel tracker was adopted by a
  12-person team in 2 weeks. Sunita rebuilt the documentation workflow over
  a weekend and the team kept it. Lavanya's Excel dashboard spread to 2
  other regional teams. Meghna's onboarding checklist became the CS team
  standard. Aditya's lost-deal write-up became a team practice.
  Weak in Vikram and Rahul, whose templates and programmes were part of
  the job they were hired for. Preetham's dashboard spread to no one.

Pattern 3 - When something broke, they stepped in themselves, kept the damage
away from the customer, and wrote down what went wrong.
  Seen in all 5 Exceeds hires. Meghna worked through the night on a customs
  hold "before the client became aware". Sunita absorbed a vendor change
  with "no disruption". Rohan migrated a data vendor "with no data loss".
  Lavanya wrote the outage post-mortem. Aditya wrote up the root cause of
  a lost deal.
  Missing in Vikram and Rahul, who list only wins. Preetham's incident work
  was an assigned on-call rotation, and he wrote nothing up.

=====================================================================
SCORING
=====================================================================
Score each criterion 0 to 5:
  5 = specific evidence (a named task, number or result) that meets every
      part of the description
  3 = evidence that meets part of the description
  0 = no evidence
Final score = sum of (score x weight) / 5, giving a result out of 100.
Quote the CV line behind every score.

=====================================================================
PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC
=====================================================================

Criterion name: Did the work before building for it
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV names shipment-level or
operations tasks the person did personally (documents prepared, holds
cleared, carriers scheduled, exceptions resolved) with a volume attached,
and their later work visibly fixes a problem from that period. Working at a
logistics company, selling to logistics customers, or connecting to
logistics systems does not count unless they did the work themselves.
Weight: 30%

Criterion name: Fixed it before anyone asked
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV says what was broken, shows
that nobody assigned it to them, and describes a rough working fix they
built quickly (a spreadsheet, checklist, or weekend prototype). Work that
was already their job or part of a planned project does not count.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Others took it up unasked
What a strong candidate looks like: Something the person made was adopted
by other people without an instruction from above, and the CV names who or
how many (e.g. "adopted by the 12-member team within two weeks"). Scores 3
at most if only the person or the team they manage used it.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Took the hit so the customer didn't
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV describes a specific moment
when something broke (a hold, outage, vendor change or lost deal), what
the person did right then, and a result stated from the customer's side
("no disruption", "before the client became aware", "no data loss").
Scores 3 at most for an assigned on-call rotation with no single incident
described.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Wrote down what went wrong
What a strong candidate looks like: The person wrote something about a
failure (a post-mortem, root-cause note, bug report, or customer-facing
incident note) and the CV says what changed because of it. A CV that lists
only wins scores 0.
Weight: 10%

PM total: 100%

=====================================================================
SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC
(same criteria, higher bar on how independently the person operated)
=====================================================================

Criterion name: Did the work before building for it
What a strong candidate looks like: Everything required at PM level,
plus the same pain shows up again in more than one later role or product,
at a bigger scope each time. Scores 3 at most for a single operations
stint with no link to later work.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Fixed it before anyone asked
What a strong candidate looks like: Everything required at PM level,
plus the person started and finished the fix alone, with no manager, team
or sponsor, and the CV says so ("independently redesigned", "built over a
weekend"). Scores 3 at most for a fix built with help or a sponsor.
Weight: 15%

Criterion name: Others took it up unasked
What a strong candidate looks like: What the person made spread beyond
their own team and became the standard way of working ("retained
permanently", "now standard practice", "the full team now uses"). Scores
3 at most if it spread only within their own team.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Took the hit so the customer didn't
What a strong candidate looks like: Everything required at PM level,
plus at least two separate incidents handled without escalating to anyone
above them ("no escalation to management"). Scores 3 at most for a single
incident, or one that was escalated.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Wrote down what went wrong
What a strong candidate looks like: The person wrote the account of a
failure themselves, it reached people beyond them (the team or the
customer), and something changed permanently because of it (e.g. a
lost-deal write-up that became the team's standard qualification step).
Weight: 15%

SPM total: 100%
